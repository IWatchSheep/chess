# Step 14: Definitions for Chess Strategy Recognition, Development Scoring, and Capture Valuation

The three missing definitions identified during the review of Steps 1–13 have been implemented and are running in the **Forming Strategies** application.

The complete implementation details are documented in `docs/definitions.md`.

The `npm test` command verifies the strategy development score categories, a fully developed example of each of the 20 strategies, and the capture valuation examples described below.

## 14.1 General Rules for Strategy Recognition

Each chess strategy is evaluated separately for White and Black.

The following rules apply:

1. Relative rank 0 represents the evaluated player's back rank, and relative rank 7 represents that player's promotion rank.
2. Strategy recognition uses geometric attacks. Therefore, a chess piece without an available legal move can still contribute to a forming strategy.
3. Capture lists consider only legal moves, and only when it is the evaluated player's turn.
4. The history window contains the evaluated player's previous four turns and the current turn when that player has just moved.
5. One full turn consists of two individual moves, also called two plies.
6. A strategy appears in the list only when its foundation, progress, or completion conditions are satisfied.
7. Future move analysis can increase a strategy's development score to 45 or 75 when the appropriate conditions occur within the next full turn.
8. A strategy completed only in a future move sequence remains classified as partially developed in the current evaluation.

## 14.2 Strategy Development Scoring

Lists 1 and 3 use the same development scoring system.

Strategies are sorted according to development stage, followed by numerical score and strategy name.

| Conditions | Development stage | Score |
|---|---|---:|
| Completion conditions satisfied now | Fully developed | 100 |
| Foundation and progress exist now, with completion possible within one full turn | Partially developed | 75 |
| Foundation and progress exist now, without completion during that turn | Partially developed | 60 |
| Foundation exists now, with progress occurring within one full turn | Beginning to form | 45 |
| Foundation only, or progress only | Beginning to form | 30 |

The sorting order is:

1. Fully developed strategies.
2. Partially developed strategies.
3. Strategies beginning to form.
4. Numerical development score, highest first.
5. Strategy name.

From the standard starting position, White's forming strategy list contains only Central Pawn Occupation and King Shelter, each with a development score of 45.

## 14.3 Chess Piece Values and Capture Calculations

The program uses the following piece values:

| Chess piece | Value |
|---|---:|
| Pawn | 100 |
| Knight | 320 |
| Bishop | 330 |
| Rook | 500 |
| Queen | 900 |

The king does not have a capture value because kings cannot legally be captured.

A comparison value of 10,000 is used only when ranking the king within the Fork and Skewer strategy rules.

### Capture Calculation

**Expected Gain:** The combined value of opposing pieces captured by the evaluated player during the exchange.

**Potential Loss:** The combined value of the evaluated player's pieces captured by the opponent during the exchange.

**Net Capture Value = Expected Gain − Potential Loss**

Checkmate adds 100,000 points to expected gain when the original player delivers checkmate, or to potential loss when the opponent's recapture delivers checkmate.

Lists 2 and 4 use the same capture valuation system.

Captures are sorted by:

1. Highest net capture value.
2. Highest expected gain.
3. Lowest potential loss.
4. Strategy name.
5. Standard Algebraic Notation (SAN).
6. Victim square.

The same legal move may appear more than once when multiple recognized strategies are associated with that capture.

### Capture Evaluation Rules

The following rules determine how capture exchanges are evaluated:

1. The initially evaluated capture is always played in the calculation.
2. Each subsequent recapture may be declined.
3. Only the cheapest legal recapturing piece is considered.
4. A pawn that promotes still counts as a pawn when determining the cheapest recapturing piece.
5. An exchange stops when continuing would produce an equal net value.
6. Promotion adds the value of the promoted piece minus 100.
7. An en passant capture assigns the captured pawn a value of 100. Subsequent recaptures target the capturing pawn's landing square.
8. A capture belongs to a recognized strategy when the capturing piece participates in that strategy and the captured piece occupies one of its target squares.
9. Kings are never treated as capture targets.

### Capture Valuation Examples

| Capture scenario | Gain | Loss | Net |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Queen captures an undefended pawn | 100 | 0 | +100 |
| Queen captures a pawn, then a knight captures the queen | 100 | 900 | −800 |
| Pawn captures a queen; the recapture would lose a rook | 900 | 0 | +900 |
| Knight captures a queen, pawn recaptures, and our pawn captures that pawn | 1,000 | 320 | +680 |
| Queen captures a rook, with a pawn as the cheaper recapturer | 500 | 900 | −400 |
| En passant followed by a rook recapture | 100 | 100 | 0 |
| Queen captures and delivers checkmate | 100,100 | 0 | +100,100 |
| Pawn promotes to a queen and the queen is captured | 800 | 900 | −100 |

## 14.4 The 20 Chess Strategies

Each strategy has conditions used to recognize its foundation, progress, and completion.

These conditions determine its development score and its position in Lists 1 and 3.

### Strategy 1: Central Pawn Occupation

- **Foundation:** A friendly pawn occupies the d-file or e-file.
- **Progress:** A friendly pawn occupies d4 or e4 for White, or d5 or e5 for Black.
- **Completion:** Both central squares are occupied by friendly pawns, and both pawns are defended.

### Strategy 2: Central Pawn Break

- **Foundation:** The opponent has a central pawn, and a friendly pawn remains on a file from c through f.
- **Progress:** A friendly pawn attacks an opposing central pawn.
- **Completion:** The turn window contains a pawn capturing another pawn on the c-, d-, e-, or f-file.

### Strategy 3: Kingside Pawn Storm

- **Foundation:** At least two friendly pawns remain on the f-, g-, or h-file, at least one has moved, and the friendly king is outside those files.
- **Progress:** A participating pawn reaches relative rank 2.
- **Completion:** Two participating pawns reach relative rank 3 or higher, and the friendly king occupies a file from a through d.

### Strategy 4: Queenside Minority Attack

- **Foundation:** The evaluated player has fewer pawns than the opponent on the a-, b-, and c-files, while retaining at least one pawn on those files.
- **Progress:** A friendly queenside pawn reaches relative rank 2.
- **Completion:** A participating pawn reaches relative rank 3, and an opposing pawn on the a- through d-files is isolated or doubled.

### Strategy 5: King Shelter

- **Foundation:** The king is on e1 or e8 with a remaining castling right, or is already on c1, g1, c8, or g8.
- **Progress:** The king has castled, or no more than one piece remains between the king and a castling rook.
- **Completion:** The king occupies a castled square, is not attacked, and its three shelter pawns remain unmoved.

### Strategy 6: Opposite-Side Castling Attack

- **Foundation:** The friendly king occupies the home rank on the a–c or f–h files, while the opposing king occupies the opposite wing.
- **Progress:** A friendly pawn on the opposing king's wing reaches relative rank 2.
- **Completion:** That pawn reaches relative rank 3, and a friendly rook or queen occupies a half-open file on that wing.

### Strategy 7: Fianchetto

- **Foundation:** The b-pawn or g-pawn has advanced one square, or a friendly bishop already occupies b2, g2, b7, or g7.
- **Progress:** A friendly bishop occupies one of those fianchetto squares.
- **Completion:** The bishop attacks at least one of the central squares d4, d5, e4, or e5.

### Strategy 8: Open-File Control

- **Foundation:** At least one file contains no friendly pawn, and a friendly rook or queen remains on the board.
- **Progress:** A friendly rook or queen occupies a file without a friendly pawn.
- **Completion:** A fully open file contains more friendly major pieces than opposing major pieces.

### Strategy 9: Seventh-Rank Invasion

- **Foundation:** A friendly rook or queen exists, and at least one file contains no friendly pawn.
- **Progress:** A friendly major piece reaches relative rank 5.
- **Completion:** A friendly rook or queen reaches the opponent's second rank and attacks an opposing piece or pawn.

### Strategy 10: Passed-Pawn Advance

- **Foundation:** A friendly pawn has no opposing pawn ahead of it on its own file or either adjacent file.
- **Progress:** The passed pawn reaches relative rank 3.
- **Completion:** The passed pawn reaches relative rank 5 or higher and is defended.

### Strategy 11: Isolated-Pawn Attack

- **Foundation:** An opposing pawn has no friendly neighboring pawn on an adjacent file.
- **Progress:** A friendly piece attacks that opposing pawn, or a friendly rook or queen shares its file.
- **Completion:** The number of attacks against the isolated pawn exceeds the number of defenses.

### Strategy 12: Backward-Pawn Pressure

- **Foundation:** An opposing pawn's advance square is attacked by a friendly pawn, is not defended by an opposing pawn, and lies on a file without a friendly pawn.
- **Progress:** A friendly rook or queen occupies the opposing pawn's file.
- **Completion:** That major piece remains on the file, and the opposing pawn's advance square is occupied or attacked.

### Strategy 13: Knight Outpost

- **Foundation:** A square on relative ranks 3 through 6 is empty or contains a friendly knight, is defended by a friendly pawn, and cannot still be attacked by an opposing pawn.
- **Progress:** A friendly knight occupies or attacks that square.
- **Completion:** A friendly knight occupies the outpost square.

### Strategy 14: Bishop Pair

- **Foundation:** The evaluated player has two bishops, while the opponent has fewer than two.
- **Progress:** At least one friendly bishop has moved away from the back rank.
- **Completion:** At least one file from c through f contains no pawns, and a friendly bishop has three squares of unobstructed travel.

### Strategy 15: Pin

- **Foundation:** A friendly bishop, rook, or queen aligns with an opposing front piece and a more valuable rear piece or king.
- **Progress:** The attacking line to the opposing front piece is unobstructed.
- **Completion:** The rear piece is the opposing king, or a second friendly piece also attacks the front piece.

### Strategy 16: Discovered Attack

- **Foundation:** Exactly one friendly piece stands between a friendly sliding piece and an opposing piece other than a pawn.
- **Progress:** The intervening friendly piece has a legal move that takes it off the attacking line.
- **Completion:** That move captures a piece or delivers check, while the sliding piece continues attacking the original target.

### Strategy 17: Fork

- **Foundation:** A piece's current square or a possible legal move creates attacks against two opposing pieces other than pawns.
- **Progress:** Two opposing target pieces are currently attacked.
- **Completion:** Both targets satisfy the strategy's value conditions.

For a fork created by a sliding piece, its targets qualify only when they are kings, undefended, or at least as valuable as the attacking piece.

### Strategy 18: Skewer

- **Foundation:** A friendly sliding piece aligns with two opposing pieces, and the nearer opposing piece is at least as valuable as the farther piece.
- **Progress:** The line to the front opposing piece is unobstructed, and the front piece is the king or is strictly more valuable than the rear piece.
- **Completion:** The front piece is the king, or it is more valuable than the attacking sliding piece while the rear piece is undefended or worth at least as much as the attacker.

### Strategy 19: Major-Piece Battery

- **Foundation:** Two friendly rooks or queens share a rank or file, no more than two pieces stand between them, and at least one participating major piece has moved from its original square.
- **Progress:** No more than one piece remains between the participating major pieces.
- **Completion:** The line between the participating pieces is clear, and they share a half-open file or one attacks an opposing piece along that line.

### Strategy 20: Promotion

- **Foundation:** A friendly pawn reaches relative rank 4 or higher.
- **Progress:** The pawn reaches relative rank 5 or higher.
- **Completion:** The pawn has an available legal promotion move.

A pawn blocked on the seventh rank remains at the progress stage until a legal promotion move becomes available.

## 14.5 Applying These Definitions to the Four Lists

The same strategy recognition and capture valuation definitions must be used consistently throughout Lists 1–4.

**List 1 — Current Forming Strategies**

Identifies and scores the strategies forming from the current board position, using the relevant move history and one full turn of future analysis.

**List 2 — Current Strategy Captures**

**List 3 — Future Forming Strategies**

Repeats the strategy recognition process one individual move into the future.

**List 4 — Future Strategy Captures**

The four lists use the same recognition and evaluation definitions to make current and future strategies comparable.
