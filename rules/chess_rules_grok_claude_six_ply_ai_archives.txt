Chess program

Step 1: When I select a chess piece, the program displays all of its available legal moves.

Step 2: Identifying pieces without legal moves

If I select a chess piece that has no available legal moves, the program displays a message explaining that the selected piece cannot currently make a legal move.

Chess pieces without available legal moves are still considered when the program identifies forming strategies. A piece's inability to move legally does not remove it from a strategy. Strategy recognition uses geometric attacks, which ignore pins, checks, and whose turn it is. Capture lists are different: they use legal moves only.

Step 3: Moving and confirming a move

If I select a chess piece that has available legal moves, I can choose a destination square by entering its two-character coordinate and pressing Enter.

Before the move is finalized, I am asked whether I want to confirm it.

If I enter "Y", the move is finalized and the piece moves to the selected square.

If I enter "N", the move is canceled and the piece remains on its original square.

The program then returns to Step 1. I can select the same piece or a different piece.

After I select a piece, the program checks its legal moves again.

1. If the selected piece has legal moves, the program displays them and I can choose a destination square.
2. If the selected piece has no legal moves, the program follows Step 2. I can then return to Step 1 and select another piece.

No change is made to the board until I confirm a legal move by entering "Y".

Step 4: Legal-move files

Before and after a move is finalized, the program lists the legal moves of every piece. Those lists are recorded in two files: one for White's turns and one for Black's turns.

Step 5: History kept in the Step 4 files

The two files from Step 4 are also the history files. Each file keeps its player's previous four turns and the current turn when that player has just moved. Each record includes the legal moves before the move was finalized and the legal moves after it was finalized. No additional history files are created.

The window used for strategy scoring is taken from this record:

- Use the evaluated player's previous four turns.
- If that player has just moved, include the turn just played. The window then holds at most five of that player's moves.
- At the start of the game the window is empty. Use as many turns as exist.

Step 6: Future search — MANDATORY THREE FULL TURNS (SIX PLIES)

The original author's requirement is authoritative. The chess program MUST examine and preserve every legal continuation through six individual moves (three moves by White and three by Black, measured from the standard opening), including all opposing responses. The previous Grok/Claude reduction to four plies is explicitly rejected and withdrawn. This six-ply requirement must not be shortened merely because Lists 1–4 directly inspect shorter portions of the tree.

At the standard starting position, the number of sequences at each depth is:

- Ply 1: 20.
- Ply 2: 400.
- Ply 3: 8,902.
- Ply 4: 197,281.
- Ply 5: 4,865,609.
- Ply 6: 119,060,324.

Total across these six depths: 124,132,536 search nodes (excluding the starting root). These counts apply to the standard starting position; other positions differ. No legal sequence may be skipped to reduce the required depth.

Two current-game future-search files are maintained: one for White-to-move roots and one for Black-to-move roots. Each file covers both players' continuations to the full six-ply horizon, updating as the current game progresses. Performance optimization and file storage may be improved without changing the set of legal sequences examined or the required depth. The distinction between searching, retaining accessible results, and physically writing every node must be documented rather than used to silently remove required information.

The four lists consume these stored results as follows:

- List 1 is evaluated on the current position, using its normal four-previous-turn/current-turn history window and one full turn of future evidence (plies 1 and 2).
- List 3 is evaluated for every legal first move at the position after ply 1; it uses the same previously generated six-ply search, with the remaining five plies available. Its normal one-full-turn strategy-scoring evidence begins at that future position; List 3 must remain tied to each candidate move and board.
- List 4 is evaluated after each candidate first move and opposing reply (after ply 2), and may use the same existing search for its scoring evidence. Captures are priced by the unchanged Step 14 exchange rules, not by adding a new root search.
- None of Lists 1–4 may restart a fresh six-ply search from a future position in a way that extends the original root horizon beyond six plies. Any unused deeper branches remain part of the required six-ply search record.

Permanent archives, if recording is enabled, preserve observed game positions throughout opening, middlegame, and endgame before rolling data is discarded. They are separate from the six-ply working search files. Archive-assisted neural predictions are advisory and must not change the original legal search, 20 strategy rules, or official development scores.

Step 7: File maintenance

New archive exception: Only the four WORKING files are cleared at a new game. The separate, permanent AI archive is never reset at game start. In opted-in games, archive every confirmed move before records leave the rolling history. See the permanent-archive requirement at the end of this document.

The two history files and the two future-search files are managed automatically. They stay organized and current as the game progresses. At the beginning of every new game, all four files are cleared and the information for the new game is generated.

Step 8: List 1, forming strategies

AI-archive addition: After applying the existing 20-strategy recognition and development-score rules, optionally attach separately labeled neural-network estimates based on complete past games. Keep original scores and ordering available.

List 1 identifies strategies that are forming for one evaluated player.

It uses:

1. That player's history window from Steps 4 and 5.
2. One full turn of future information from that player's Step 6 file.
3. The 20 strategies and the scoring rules in Step 14.

A strategy is listed only when its foundation, its progress, or its completion is already true on the position being scored. A pattern that exists only on a future move is not a row. Future evidence can raise a listed score to 45 or 75. It cannot raise a score to 100.

Step 9: Sorting List 1

List 1 is sorted by development stage, then by score, then by name:

1. Fully developed.
2. Partially developed.
3. Beginning to form.
4. Higher score before lower score.
5. Strategy name, A to Z.

Step 10: List 2, captures for List 1

List 2 records the legal captures associated with the strategies in List 1.

List 1 and List 2 are computed together, on the same position. List 2 contains rows only when it is the evaluated player's turn to move. When it is the opponent's turn, List 2 is empty. It is not postponed until that player's next turn.

For each capture, List 2 records:

1. The strategy name.
2. The evaluated player's piece that captures.
3. The opposing piece that can be captured.
4. The square of the capturing piece and the square of the opposing piece.
5. The expected gain, the potential loss, and the net value from Step 14.

Potential loss is the total value of the evaluated player's pieces the opponent takes in that exchange. When the capturing piece is recaptured, its value is included. Later pieces of that player taken on the same square are included too.

A strategy with no associated capture remains in List 1 and adds no List 2 row.

Step 11: Sorting List 2

List 2 is sorted by:

1. Highest net value.
2. Highest expected gain.
3. Lowest potential loss.
4. Strategy name, A to Z.
5. Standard Algebraic Notation of the move.
6. Victim square.

The same legal move is repeated once for each strategy that claims it.

Step 12: List 3, strategies after the next move

AI-archive addition: For each legal next move, optionally attach archive-informed neural predictions of strategy progress and outcomes. Keep each estimate tied to its candidate move, use the same stored future search, and do not alter the official development score.

List 3 is computed only when it is the evaluated player's turn to move.

For every legal move by that player:

1. Play that move.
2. On the resulting position, repeat the List 1 recognition and scoring from Steps 8 and 9.
3. Keep the result tied to that move. Do not merge every future move into one list.
4. Read the future evidence from the search already stored by Step 6. Do not start another search.

On that position the opponent is to move. The one full turn used by the score is the opponent's reply and the evaluated player's move after that reply.

Step 13: List 4, captures after the opponent's reply

List 4 is two individual moves ahead of the position on which List 1 was built. It is not one move ahead.

For every List 3 move, and for every legal reply by the opponent to that move:

1. Play the reply.
2. It is then the evaluated player's turn again.
3. Repeat strategy recognition, scoring, and capture pricing on that position. Use Steps 8 through 11.
4. Keep the result tied to the first move and to the reply.

The strategies are recognized again after the reply. They are not copied unchanged from List 3, because the reply can create or remove a pattern. The development score uses one full turn beyond the reply, which is individual moves 3 and 4 of the original search.

The capture rows use the Step 14 exchange. That exchange stays on the capture square.


Step 14: Strategy recognition, development scoring, and capture valuation

14.1 How a position is read

Each strategy is evaluated for one player at a time. That player is the evaluated player. "Friendly" means that player's pieces. "Opposing" means the other player's pieces.

1. Relative rank. Relative rank 0 is the evaluated player's back rank. Relative rank 7 is that player's promotion rank. A white pawn starts on relative rank 1. Relative rank 6 is the opponent's second rank: rank 7 for White, rank 2 for Black. Every rank test in the 20 strategies uses this scale.

2. Central pawn. A central pawn is a pawn standing on d4, d5, e4, or e5. A pawn on d2, d7, e2, or e7 is not a central pawn.

3. Geometric attack. A piece attacks a square by its move shape. Pawns attack one square diagonally forward. Sliding pieces stop at the first piece. Pins, checks, and whose turn it is do not matter. A piece with no legal move still attacks geometrically.

4. Defended. A piece or square is defended when at least one friendly piece attacks it geometrically.

5. Legal move. A move the rules allow on that player's turn, including king safety. Each promotion choice is its own legal move. When it is not the evaluated player's turn, the question "does this piece have a legal move" is answered by generating moves as if the opponent passed. Capture lists do not use that pass. A capture is priced only when it is actually the evaluated player's turn.

6. One full turn. Two individual moves: a move and the reply. If the evaluated player is to move, the turn is that player's move and the opponent's reply. If the opponent is to move, the turn is the opponent's move and the evaluated player's reply. The search stops when a line ends in checkmate.

7. "Within one full turn" is existential. It means at least one legal line, not every reply. After each of the two positions in a line, the strategy is read again. Progress-within-one-full-turn is true if any of those positions has progress or completion. Completion-within-one-full-turn is true if any of those positions has completion. The condition does not have to survive every opposing reply.

8. A strategy is listed only when foundation, progress, or completion is already true on the position being scored. Future evidence raises a listed score. It does not create a row by itself, and it does not make the strategy fully developed.

9. Half-open file. A file with no friendly pawn. Fully open file. A file with no pawn of either color. Doubled pawns. Two or more pawns of the same color on one file. Isolated pawn. A pawn with no pawn of its own color on an adjacent file.

10. Sliding piece. A bishop, rook, or queen. Major piece. A rook or queen.

14.3 Piece values and capture valuation

| Piece | Value |
| --- | ---: |
| Pawn | 100 |
| Knight | 320 |
| Bishop | 330 |
| Rook | 500 |
| Queen | 900 |

The bishop is worth 10 more than the knight, so a bishop-for-knight trade is not a tie.

The king has no capture value. Kings are never capture targets. The comparison value 10,000 is used only to treat the king as heavier than every other piece inside Fork and Skewer. It is never added to expected gain or potential loss.

Expected gain is the total value of opposing pieces the evaluated player takes in the priced sequence.

Potential loss is the total value of the evaluated player's own pieces the opponent takes in that same sequence.

Net value is expected gain minus potential loss.

Checkmate adds 100,000 to expected gain when the evaluated player mates, or to potential loss when the opponent's recapture mates.

Lists 2 and 4 contain captures only. A promotion that does not capture is not a row. The promotion adjustment below still applies when a capture also promotes.

Exchange rules

1. The capture being priced is always played, even when the net value is negative.
2. Every recapture after that capture may be declined.
3. Recaptures are legal captures onto the capture square. An en passant victim is a pawn worth 100, and later recaptures aim at the square where the capturing pawn landed.
4. Only the cheapest standing piece value is tried. A pawn is worth 100 for this comparison even if that pawn will promote. A king has standing value 10,000, so a king is tried only when no cheaper legal recapture exists. When several pieces share the cheapest value, each is tried.
5. A side recaptures only when the recapture is strictly better for that side than stopping. The evaluated player recaptures only when the net value rises. The opponent recaptures only when the evaluated player's net value falls. An equal net value means that side stops. "Equal" compares the net value after the recapture, including the rest of the exchange, with the net value of stopping now.
6. The side about to move keeps the result that is best for it under rule 5. The evaluated player maximizes net value. The opponent minimizes the evaluated player's net value.
7. A pawn that promotes adds the new piece's value minus 100 to the side that promoted. The piece then standing on the square is the promoted piece. A later recapture loses the queen, rook, bishop, or knight, not the pawn.
8. If the original capture checkmates, add 100,000 to expected gain and stop. Potential loss stays 0. If a recapture checkmates, add 100,000 to expected gain when the evaluated player mates, or to potential loss when the opponent mates, and stop.
9. A capture belongs to a strategy only when the moving piece is one of that strategy's participating pieces and the captured piece stands on one of that strategy's target squares.

Checked exchanges

1. Queen takes an undefended pawn.

White queen d1, white king e1, black pawn d5, black king e8. Qxd5. Nothing recaptures. Expected gain 100. Potential loss 0. Net +100.

2. Queen takes a pawn and a knight recaptures the queen.

The same position, with a black knight on e7. Qxd5, then Nxd5. Expected gain 100. Potential loss 900. Net -800.

3. Pawn takes a queen, and the opponent declines the recapture.

White pawn d4, white rook e1, white king g1. Black queen e5, black rook e8, black king h8. The priced move is dxe5, which gains the queen, 900.

Black's only legal recapture on e5 is Rxe5. After that recapture White plays Rxe5 and gains the rook as well. The continued line is worth 1,400 of gain and 100 of loss, a net of +1,300 for White. Stopping is worth +900 for White. The opponent recaptures only when White's net falls. 900 is lower than 1,300, so Black stops. The rook is not lost, because the recapture is declined. Expected gain 900. Potential loss 0. Net +900.

4. Knight takes a queen, the pawn recaptures, and our pawn takes that pawn.

White knight g4, white pawn g5, white king e1. Black queen f6, black pawn e7, black king e8. The move is Nxf6+.

If Black stops, White's net is +900. Black plays exf6. If White then stops, White's net is +580. White instead plays gxf6, and the net is +680. White recaptures because 680 is higher than 580. Black recaptures because 680 is lower than 900. Expected gain 1,000. Potential loss 320. Net +680.

5. Queen takes a rook, and the cheaper pawn recaptures.

White queen d1, white king e1. Black rook d5, black pawn c6, black queen d8, black king h8. Qxd5 gains the rook, 500. Both the pawn on c6 and the queen on d8 attack d5. The pawn is cheaper, so cxd5 is tried and the queen recapture is not. Expected gain 500. Potential loss 900. Net -400.

6. En passant, then the rook recaptures.

White pawn e5, white king e1. Black pawn d5, black rook d8, black king e8. En passant is legal. exd6 captures the pawn on d5, and the white pawn lands on d6. Stopping would leave White's net at +100. Rxd6 leaves the net at 0, which is strictly lower for White, so Black recaptures. Expected gain 100. Potential loss 100. Net 0.

7. Queen captures and checkmates.

White queen b6, white knight c5, white king e1. Black king a8, black pawn b7. Qxb7# gains the pawn and checkmates. Expected gain 100,100. Potential loss 0. Net +100,100.

8. Pawn captures, promotes to a queen, and the new queen is recaptured.

White pawn g7, white king e1. Black knight f8, black rook a8, black king g8. gxf8=Q+ captures the knight and promotes. The gain is the knight, 320, plus the promotion adjustment, 900 - 100 = 800, for 1,120. The king on g8 also attacks f8, but the rook on a8 is the cheaper recapturer, so Rxf8 is tried and the king is not. Stopping would leave White's net at +1,120. After Rxf8 the net is +220, so Black recaptures. Expected gain 1,120. Potential loss 900. Net +220.

A promotion that does not capture is priced by the same arithmetic and is not placed in List 2 or List 4. White pawn f7, white king e1, black rook h8, black king h7, with f8 empty: f8=Q, then Rxf8. Expected gain 800. Potential loss 900. Net -100. That move is absent from the capture lists because it captures nothing.

14.4 The 20 strategies

Participating pieces may make a strategy's capture. Targets are the opposing pieces those captures may take. Kings are never targets.

Strategy 1: Central Pawn Occupation

Occupy d4 and e4, or d5 and e5, with pawns that are defended.

- Foundation. At least one friendly pawn stands on the d-file or the e-file.
- Progress. A friendly pawn occupies d4 or e4 for White, or d5 or e5 for Black.
- Completion. Friendly pawns occupy both of those squares, and each pawn is defended.
- Captures. The capturer is a central pawn or a piece that defends one. The victim stands on c4, c5, d4, d5, e4, e5, f4, or f5, or is a pawn that attacks a friendly central pawn.

Strategy 2: Central Pawn Break

Challenge an opposing central pawn by a pawn capture on the c-, d-, e-, or f-file.

- Foundation. The opponent has a pawn on d4, d5, e4, or e5, and a friendly pawn remains on a file from c through f. Pawns on d2, d7, e2, or e7 do not satisfy this foundation.
- Progress. A friendly pawn geometrically attacks an opposing pawn on d4, d5, e4, or e5.
- Completion. The history window contains a friendly pawn capturing a pawn onto the c-, d-, e-, or f-file. An empty window cannot complete this strategy. Loading a position without its move history leaves this strategy incomplete until such a capture is recorded.
- Captures. The capturer is a pawn on a file from c through f. The victim is an opposing central pawn that this pawn attacks.

Strategy 3: Kingside Pawn Storm

Advance the f-, g-, and h-pawns while the friendly king stays off that wing.

- Foundation. At least two friendly pawns remain on the f-, g-, and h-files, at least one of them has left its starting square, and the friendly king is not on the f-, g-, or h-file.
- Progress. A pawn on the f-, g-, or h-file has relative rank 2 or higher, and the friendly king is still off those files.
- Completion. At least two of those pawns have relative rank 3 or higher, and the friendly king is on a file from a through d.
- Captures. The capturer is a storm pawn or a piece that attacks the f-, g-, or h-file. The victim stands on one of those files.

Strategy 4: Queenside Minority Attack

Advance a smaller queenside pawn mass until the opponent has an isolated or doubled pawn.

- Foundation. On the a-, b-, and c-files the opponent has strictly more pawns than the evaluated player, the evaluated player has at least one pawn on those files, and the opponent has a pawn on a file from a through d.
- Progress. A friendly pawn on the a-, b-, or c-file has relative rank 2 or higher.
- Completion. A friendly pawn on the a-, b-, or c-file has relative rank 3 or higher, and an opposing pawn on a file from a through d is isolated or doubled.
- Captures. The capturer is a pawn on the a-, b-, or c-file, or a rook or queen on a file from a through d. The victim is an opposing pawn on a file from a through d.

Strategy 5: King Shelter

Castle, and keep a pawn on each of the three shelter squares in front of the king.

- Foundation. The king is on e1 or e8 and at least one castling right remains, or the king is already on c1, g1, c8, or g8.
- Progress. The king is on c1, g1, c8, or g8, or, for a wing whose castling right remains, at most one piece stands between the king and that wing's rook.
- Completion. The king is on g1, g8, c1, or c8, the king is not geometrically attacked, and all three shelter squares of that wing contain friendly pawns. The kingside squares are f2, g2, and h2 for White, or f7, g7, and h7 for Black. The queenside squares are a2, b2, and c2 for White, or a7, b7, and c7 for Black.
- Captures. The capturer is the king, a shelter pawn, or a rook on the home rank. The victim attacks the king or a shelter pawn.

King Shelter and Fianchetto oppose each other on the same wing. This is intended. A kingside fianchetto moves the g-pawn off g2 or g7, so the kingside shelter cannot be complete. A queenside fianchetto likewise prevents queenside shelter completion. The other wing is unaffected. Both strategies may be listed at the same time. They cannot both be complete on the same wing.

Strategy 6: Opposite-Side Castling Attack

Storm the wing where the opposing king stands after the kings are on opposite wings.

- Foundation. The friendly king is on its home rank on the a-, b-, or c-file and the opposing king is on the f-, g-, or h-file, or the friendly king is on its home rank on the g- or h-file and the opposing king is on the a-, b-, or c-file.
- Progress. A friendly pawn on the opposing king's wing has relative rank 2 or higher. The opposing king's wing is the f-, g-, and h-files, or the a-, b-, and c-files, according to the foundation that matched.
- Completion. One of those pawns has relative rank 3 or higher, and a friendly rook or queen stands on a half-open file of that wing.
- Captures. The capturer is a pawn of that wing or a rook or queen on that wing. The victim stands on that wing.

Strategy 7: Fianchetto

Place a bishop on the long diagonal behind a pawn that has advanced one square, and aim the bishop at the center.

- Foundation. The b-pawn or the g-pawn has moved exactly one square, or the bishop already stands on b2, g2, b7, or g7, and a friendly bishop of the matching square color still exists.
- Progress. That bishop occupies the fianchetto square.
- Completion. That bishop geometrically attacks d4, d5, e4, or e5.
- Captures. The capturer is the fianchetto bishop or the pawn that opened its diagonal. The victim is a piece the bishop attacks.

Strategy 8: Open-File Control

Put a rook or queen on a file that contains no friendly pawn, and hold a fully open file.

- Foundation. Some file contains no friendly pawn, and a friendly rook or queen remains on the board.
- Progress. A friendly rook or queen stands on a file that contains no friendly pawn.
- Completion. A fully open file contains more friendly rooks and queens than opposing rooks and queens.
- Captures. The capturer is a rook or queen on a half-open file. The victim is a piece that major piece attacks.

Strategy 9: Seventh-Rank Invasion

Bring a rook or queen to relative rank 6 and attack an opposing unit there.

- Foundation. A friendly rook or queen exists, and at least one file contains no friendly pawn.
- Progress. A friendly rook or queen has relative rank 5 or higher.
- Completion. A friendly rook or queen has relative rank 6 and geometrically attacks an opposing unit. Relative rank 6 is White's seventh rank and Black's second rank.
- Captures. The capturer is a rook or queen on relative rank 5 or higher. The victim is a piece that piece attacks.

Strategy 10: Passed-Pawn Advance

Create a passed pawn, advance it, and defend it.

- Foundation. A friendly pawn has no opposing pawn on its file, or on an adjacent file, in front of it.
- Progress. That passed pawn has relative rank 3 or higher.
- Completion. A passed pawn has relative rank 5 or higher and is defended.
- Captures. The capturer is the passed pawn or a piece that defends it. The victim blocks it, attacks it, or stands on the square in front of it.

Strategy 11: Isolated-Pawn Attack

Attack an opposing pawn that has no neighbor of its own color.

- Foundation. The opponent has a pawn with no pawn of the opponent's own color on an adjacent file. The evaluated player's pawns do not decide whether that pawn is isolated.
- Progress. A friendly unit geometrically attacks that pawn, or a friendly rook or queen shares its file.
- Completion. The isolated pawn is geometrically attacked more times than it is defended.
- Captures. The capturer attacks the isolated pawn, or is a rook or queen sharing its file. The victim is the isolated pawn or a piece that defends it.

Strategy 12: Backward-Pawn Pressure

Restrain a pawn whose advance square is hit by a friendly pawn.

- Foundation. An opposing pawn's next square forward is attacked by a friendly pawn, no opposing pawn defends that advance square, and the pawn's file contains no friendly pawn.
- Progress. A friendly rook or queen shares that pawn's file.
- Completion. The advance square is occupied or attacked by the evaluated player, and the rook or queen remains on the file.
- Captures. The capturer is that major piece or a unit that attacks the advance square. The victim is the backward pawn or a piece that defends it.

Strategy 13: Knight Outpost

A knight occupies a square, on relative ranks 3 through 6, that a friendly pawn defends and no opposing pawn can attack.

- Foundation. Such a square exists. It is empty or already occupied by a friendly knight. An opposing pawn can still attack the square when that pawn stands on an adjacent file and its relative rank, measured for the opponent, is lower than the square's relative rank.
- Progress. A friendly knight occupies the square or geometrically attacks it.
- Completion. A friendly knight occupies the square.
- Captures. The capturer is the occupying knight or the pawn that defends the square. The victim is a piece the occupying knight attacks.

Strategy 14: Bishop Pair

Keep both bishops against fewer opposing bishops, and open a central file for them.

- Foundation. The evaluated player has two bishops and the opponent has fewer than two.
- Progress. At least one friendly bishop has left relative rank 0.
- Completion. A file from c through f contains no pawns, and a friendly bishop has at least three empty squares of travel along one single direction. The count is the longest one ray. It stops at the first occupied square. It is not the sum of every direction.
- Captures. The capturer is one of the two bishops. The victim is a piece that bishop attacks.

Strategy 15: Pin

A bishop, rook, or queen attacks an opposing piece that has a heavier opposing piece or the opposing king directly behind it.

Walk each ray from the sliding piece. Pieces are met in order. A candidate pair is two opposing pieces with no piece between them. The nearer is the front piece. The farther is the rear piece. The rear piece qualifies when it is the king or when it is strictly more valuable than the front piece.

- Foundation. A qualifying pair exists, and the stretch from the sliding piece to the front piece contains either no piece, or exactly one piece and that piece is opposing. If the first piece on the ray is friendly, that ray contains no pin. The rook on a1, the white pawn on a2, the black pawn on a7, and the rook on a8 are not a pin.
- Progress. No piece stands between the sliding piece and the front piece. The sliding piece geometrically attacks the front piece.
- Completion. Progress is true, and either the rear piece is the king or a second friendly unit also attacks the front piece.
- Captures. The capturer is the pinning piece or the second attacker. The victim is the front piece.

A friendly piece standing between the sliding piece and an opposing target is a discovered attack when Strategy 16 matches. It is not a pin.

Strategy 16: Discovered Attack

A friendly piece stands between a friendly sliding piece and an opposing target, and can move off that line.

- Foundation. The first piece on the sliding piece's ray is friendly, and the second piece on that ray is an opposing piece other than a pawn.
- Progress. That screening piece has a legal move that leaves the line.
- Completion. At least one such legal move captures or gives check, and after that move the sliding piece still attacks the original target. The score of 100 means the move is available on the position being scored. The move does not already have to have been played.
- Captures. The capturer is the sliding piece or the screening piece. The victim is the discovered target.

Strategy 17: Fork

One piece attacks two opposing pieces other than pawns, or a legal move reaches a square from which it would.

The two targets used for the value test are the two highest-valued opposing non-pawns attacked by that same piece. A target the moving piece would capture on the move being considered is not a target, because it would no longer stand there. A target meets the value test when it is a king, or it is not defended, or its value is at least the attacker's value.

- Foundation. One piece currently attacks two opposing non-pawns, or a legal move reaches a square from which that piece would attack two opposing non-pawns. For a bishop, rook, or queen, both targets must already meet the value test. For a knight or a pawn, any two opposing non-pawns are enough for the foundation.
- Progress. The same piece currently attacks both targets. A fork that exists only on a legal move to another square is foundation without progress. For a sliding piece, progress also requires the value test.
- Completion. The piece currently attacks both targets, and both meet the value test. This is the completion rule for knights and pawns as well as for sliding pieces. A knight is worth 320 and a pawn is worth 100. Every piece other than a pawn is worth at least 320. Therefore a knight or a pawn that currently attacks two opposing non-pawns is complete.
- Captures. The capturer is the forking piece. The victim is one of the forked pieces.

Strategy 18: Skewer

A sliding piece attacks a more valuable piece, with a lesser opposing piece directly behind it.

Use the same ray rule as Strategy 15. The front piece's value is at least the rear piece's value.

- Foundation. Such a pair exists, under the pin ray rule: the two opposing pieces are adjacent on the ray, and the stretch in front of the nearer piece is empty or contains exactly one opposing piece. A friendly first piece on the ray blocks the skewer.
- Progress. No piece stands between the sliding piece and the front piece, and the front piece is the king or is strictly more valuable than the rear piece.
- Completion. Progress is true, and either the front piece is the king, or the front piece is worth more than the sliding piece and the rear piece is undefended or worth at least as much as the sliding piece.
- Captures. The capturer is the sliding piece. The victim is the front piece or the rear piece.

Strategy 19: Major-Piece Battery

Two friendly rooks, or a friendly rook and queen, share a file or a rank.

- Foundation. The two pieces share a file or a rank, at most two pieces stand between them, and at least one of them has left its original square.
- Progress. At most one piece stands between them.
- Completion. Nothing stands between them, and they share a half-open file or one of them attacks an opposing piece along that line.
- Captures. The capturer is one of the two pieces. The victim is a piece that piece attacks.

Step 6: Future search — MANDATORY THREE FULL TURNS (SIX PLIES)

The original author's requirement is authoritative. The chess program MUST examine and preserve every legal continuation through six individual moves (three moves by White and three by Black, measured from the standard opening), including all opposing responses. The previous Grok/Claude reduction to four plies is explicitly rejected and withdrawn. This six-ply requirement must not be shortened merely because Lists 1–4 directly inspect shorter portions of the tree.

At the standard starting position, the number of sequences at each depth is:

- Ply 1: 20.
- Ply 2: 400.
- Ply 3: 8,902.
- Ply 4: 197,281.
- Ply 5: 4,865,609.
- Ply 6: 119,060,324.

Total across these six depths: 124,132,536 search nodes (excluding the starting root). These counts apply to the standard starting position; other positions differ. No legal sequence may be skipped to reduce the required depth.

Two current-game future-search files are maintained: one for White-to-move roots and one for Black-to-move roots. Each file covers both players' continuations to the full six-ply horizon, updating as the current game progresses. Performance optimization and file storage may be improved without changing the set of legal sequences examined or the required depth. The distinction between searching, retaining accessible results, and physically writing every node must be documented rather than used to silently remove required information.

The four lists consume these stored results as follows:

- List 1 is evaluated on the current position, using its normal four-previous-turn/current-turn history window and one full turn of future evidence (plies 1 and 2).
- List 3 is evaluated for every legal first move at the position after ply 1; it uses the same previously generated six-ply search, with the remaining five plies available. Its normal one-full-turn strategy-scoring evidence begins at that future position; List 3 must remain tied to each candidate move and board.
- List 4 is evaluated after each candidate first move and opposing reply (after ply 2), and may use the same existing search for its scoring evidence. Captures are priced by the unchanged Step 14 exchange rules, not by adding a new root search.
- None of Lists 1–4 may restart a fresh six-ply search from a future position in a way that extends the original root horizon beyond six plies. Any unused deeper branches remain part of the required six-ply search record.

Permanent archives, if recording is enabled, preserve observed game positions throughout opening, middlegame, and endgame before rolling data is discarded. They are separate from the six-ply working search files. Archive-assisted neural predictions are advisory and must not change the original legal search, 20 strategy rules, or official development scores.

The two history files and the two future-search files are managed automatically. They stay organized and current as the game progresses. At the beginning of every new game, all four files are cleared and the information for the new game is generated.

Step 14: Strategy recognition, development scoring, and capture valuation

14.1 How a position is read

Each strategy is evaluated for one player at a time. That player is the evaluated player. "Friendly" means that player's pieces. "Opposing" means the other player's pieces.

1. Relative rank. Relative rank 0 is the evaluated player's back rank. Relative rank 7 is that player's promotion rank. A white pawn starts on relative rank 1. Relative rank 6 is the opponent's second rank: rank 7 for White, rank 2 for Black. Every rank test in the 20 strategies uses this scale.

2. Central pawn. A central pawn is a pawn standing on d4, d5, e4, or e5. A pawn on d2, d7, e2, or e7 is not a central pawn.

3. Geometric attack. A piece attacks a square by its move shape. Pawns attack one square diagonally forward. Sliding pieces stop at the first piece. Pins, checks, and whose turn it is do not matter. A piece with no legal move still attacks geometrically.

4. Defended. A piece or square is defended when at least one friendly piece attacks it geometrically.

5. Legal move. A move the rules allow on that player's turn, including king safety. Each promotion choice is its own legal move. When it is not the evaluated player's turn, the question "does this piece have a legal move" is answered by generating moves as if the opponent passed. Capture lists do not use that pass. A capture is priced only when it is actually the evaluated player's turn.

6. One full turn. Two individual moves: a move and the reply. If the evaluated player is to move, the turn is that player's move and the opponent's reply. If the opponent is to move, the turn is the opponent's move and the evaluated player's reply. The search stops when a line ends in checkmate.

7. "Within one full turn" is existential. It means at least one legal line, not every reply. After each of the two positions in a line, the strategy is read again. Progress-within-one-full-turn is true if any of those positions has progress or completion. Completion-within-one-full-turn is true if any of those positions has completion. The condition does not have to survive every opposing reply.

8. A strategy is listed only when foundation, progress, or completion is already true on the position being scored. Future evidence raises a listed score. It does not create a row by itself, and it does not make the strategy fully developed.

9. Half-open file. A file with no friendly pawn. Fully open file. A file with no pawn of either color. Doubled pawns. Two or more pawns of the same color on one file. Isolated pawn. A pawn with no pawn of its own color on an adjacent file.

10. Sliding piece. A bishop, rook, or queen. Major piece. A rook or queen.

14.3 Piece values and capture valuation

| Piece | Value |
| --- | ---: |
| Pawn | 100 |
| Knight | 320 |
| Bishop | 330 |
| Rook | 500 |
| Queen | 900 |

The bishop is worth 10 more than the knight, so a bishop-for-knight trade is not a tie.

The king has no capture value. Kings are never capture targets. The comparison value 10,000 is used only to treat the king as heavier than every other piece inside Fork and Skewer. It is never added to expected gain or potential loss.

Expected gain is the total value of opposing pieces the evaluated player takes in the priced sequence.

Potential loss is the total value of the evaluated player's own pieces the opponent takes in that same sequence.

Net value is expected gain minus potential loss.

Checkmate adds 100,000 to expected gain when the evaluated player mates, or to potential loss when the opponent's recapture mates.

Lists 2 and 4 contain captures only. A promotion that does not capture is not a row. The promotion adjustment below still applies when a capture also promotes.

Exchange rules

1. The capture being priced is always played, even when the net value is negative.
2. Every recapture after that capture may be declined.
3. Recaptures are legal captures onto the capture square. An en passant victim is a pawn worth 100, and later recaptures aim at the square where the capturing pawn landed.
4. Only the cheapest standing piece value is tried. A pawn is worth 100 for this comparison even if that pawn will promote. A king has standing value 10,000, so a king is tried only when no cheaper legal recapture exists. When several pieces share the cheapest value, each is tried.
5. A side recaptures only when the recapture is strictly better for that side than stopping. The evaluated player recaptures only when the net value rises. The opponent recaptures only when the evaluated player's net value falls. An equal net value means that side stops. "Equal" compares the net value after the recapture, including the rest of the exchange, with the net value of stopping now.
6. The side about to move keeps the result that is best for it under rule 5. The evaluated player maximizes net value. The opponent minimizes the evaluated player's net value.
7. A pawn that promotes adds the new piece's value minus 100 to the side that promoted. The piece then standing on the square is the promoted piece. A later recapture loses the queen, rook, bishop, or knight, not the pawn.
8. If the original capture checkmates, add 100,000 to expected gain and stop. Potential loss stays 0. If a recapture checkmates, add 100,000 to expected gain when the evaluated player mates, or to potential loss when the opponent mates, and stop.
9. A capture belongs to a strategy only when the moving piece is one of that strategy's participating pieces and the captured piece stands on one of that strategy's target squares.

Checked exchanges

1. Queen takes an undefended pawn.

White queen d1, white king e1, black pawn d5, black king e8. Qxd5. Nothing recaptures. Expected gain 100. Potential loss 0. Net +100.

2. Queen takes a pawn and a knight recaptures the queen.

The same position, with a black knight on e7. Qxd5, then Nxd5. Expected gain 100. Potential loss 900. Net −800.

3. Pawn takes a queen, and the opponent declines the recapture.

White pawn d4, white rook e1, white king g1. Black queen e5, black rook e8, black king h8. The priced move is dxe5, which gains the queen, 900.

Black's only legal recapture on e5 is Rxe5. After that recapture White plays Rxe5 and gains the rook as well. The continued line is worth 1,400 of gain and 100 of loss, a net of +1,300 for White. Stopping is worth +900 for White. The opponent recaptures only when White's net falls. 900 is lower than 1,300, so Black stops. The rook is not lost, because the recapture is declined. Expected gain 900. Potential loss 0. Net +900.

4. Knight takes a queen, the pawn recaptures, and our pawn takes that pawn.

White knight g4, white pawn g5, white king e1. Black queen f6, black pawn e7, black king e8. The move is Nxf6+.

If Black stops, White's net is +900. Black plays exf6. If White then stops, White's net is +580. White instead plays gxf6, and the net is +680. White recaptures because 680 is higher than 580. Black recaptures because 680 is lower than 900. Expected gain 1,000. Potential loss 320. Net +680.

5. Queen takes a rook, and the cheaper pawn recaptures.

White queen d1, white king e1. Black rook d5, black pawn c6, black queen d8, black king h8. Qxd5 gains the rook, 500. Both the pawn on c6 and the queen on d8 attack d5. The pawn is cheaper, so cxd5 is tried and the queen recapture is not. Expected gain 500. Potential loss 900. Net −400.

6. En passant, then the rook recaptures.

White pawn e5, white king e1. Black pawn d5, black rook d8, black king e8. En passant is legal. exd6 captures the pawn on d5, and the white pawn lands on d6. Stopping would leave White's net at +100. Rxd6 leaves the net at 0, which is strictly lower for White, so Black recaptures. Expected gain 100. Potential loss 100. Net 0.

7. Queen captures and checkmates.

White queen b6, white knight c5, white king e1. Black king a8, black pawn b7. Qxb7# gains the pawn and checkmates. Expected gain 100,100. Potential loss 0. Net +100,100.

8. Pawn captures, promotes to a queen, and the new queen is recaptured.

White pawn g7, white king e1. Black knight f8, black rook a8, black king g8. gxf8=Q+ captures the knight and promotes. The gain is the knight, 320, plus the promotion adjustment, 900 − 100 = 800, for 1,120. The king on g8 also attacks f8, but the rook on a8 is the cheaper recapturer, so Rxf8 is tried and the king is not. Stopping would leave White's net at +1,120. After Rxf8 the net is +220, so Black recaptures. Expected gain 1,120. Potential loss 900. Net +220.

A promotion that does not capture is priced by the same arithmetic and is not placed in List 2 or List 4. White pawn f7, white king e1, black rook h8, black king h7, with f8 empty: f8=Q, then Rxf8. 14.4 The 20 strategies

Participating pieces may make a strategy's capture. Targets are the opposing pieces those captures may take. Kings are never targets.

Strategy 1: Central Pawn Occupation

Occupy d4 and e4, or d5 and e5, with pawns that are defended.

- Foundation. At least one friendly pawn stands on the d-file or the e-file.
- Progress. A friendly pawn occupies d4 or e4 for White, or d5 or e5 for Black.
- Completion. Friendly pawns occupy both of those squares, and each pawn is defended.
- Captures. The capturer is a central pawn or a piece that defends one. The victim stands on c4, c5, d4, d5, e4, e5, f4, or f5, or is a pawn that attacks a friendly central pawn.

Strategy 2: Central Pawn Break

Challenge an opposing central pawn by a pawn capture on the c-, d-, e-, or f-file.

- Foundation. The opponent has a pawn on d4, d5, e4, or e5, and a friendly pawn remains on a file from c through f. Pawns on d2, d7, e2, or e7 do not satisfy this foundation.
- Progress. A friendly pawn geometrically attacks an opposing pawn on d4, d5, e4, or e5.
- Completion. The history window contains a friendly pawn capturing a pawn onto the c-, d-, e-, or f-file. An empty window cannot complete this strategy. Loading a position without its move history leaves this strategy incomplete until such a capture is recorded.
- Captures. The capturer is a pawn on a file from c through f. The victim is an opposing central pawn that this pawn attacks.

Strategy 3: Kingside Pawn Storm

Advance the f-, g-, and h-pawns while the friendly king stays off that wing.

- Foundation. At least two friendly pawns remain on the f-, g-, and h-files, at least one of them has left its starting square, and the friendly king is not on the f-, g-, or h-file.
- Progress. A pawn on the f-, g-, or h-file has relative rank 2 or higher, and the friendly king is still off those files.
- Completion. At least two of those pawns have relative rank 3 or higher, and the friendly king is on a file from a through d.
- Captures. The capturer is a storm pawn or a piece that attacks the f-, g-, or h-file. The victim stands on one of those files.

Strategy 4: Queenside Minority Attack

Advance a smaller queenside pawn mass until the opponent has an isolated or doubled pawn.

- Foundation. On the a-, b-, and c-files the opponent has strictly more pawns than the evaluated player, the evaluated player has at least one pawn on those files, and the opponent has a pawn on a file from a through d.
- Progress. A friendly pawn on the a-, b-, or c-file has relative rank 2 or higher.
- Completion. A friendly pawn on the a-, b-, or c-file has relative rank 3 or higher, and an opposing pawn on a file from a through d is isolated or doubled.
- Captures. The capturer is a pawn on the a-, b-, or c-file, or a rook or queen on a file from a through d. The victim is an opposing pawn on a file from a through d.

Strategy 5: King Shelter

Castle, and keep a pawn on each of the three shelter squares in front of the king.

- Foundation. The king is on e1 or e8 and at least one castling right remains, or the king is already on c1, g1, c8, or g8.
- Progress. The king is on c1, g1, c8, or g8, or, for a wing whose castling right remains, at most one piece stands between the king and that wing's rook.
- Completion. The king is on g1, g8, c1, or c8, the king is not geometrically attacked, and all three shelter squares of that wing contain friendly pawns. The kingside squares are f2, g2, and h2 for White, or f7, g7, and h7 for Black. The queenside squares are a2, b2, and c2 for White, or a7, b7, and c7 for Black.
- Captures. The capturer is the king, a shelter pawn, or a rook on the home rank. The victim attacks the king or a shelter pawn.

King Shelter and Fianchetto oppose each other on the same wing. This is intended. A kingside fianchetto moves the g-pawn off g2 or g7, so the kingside shelter cannot be complete. A queenside fianchetto likewise prevents queenside shelter completion. The other wing is unaffected. Both strategies may be listed at the same time. They cannot both be complete on the same wing.

Strategy 6: Opposite-Side Castling Attack

Storm the wing where the opposing king stands after the kings are on opposite wings.

- Foundation. The friendly king is on its home rank on the a-, b-, or c-file and the opposing king is on the f-, g-, or h-file, or the friendly king is on its home rank on the g- or h-file and the opposing king is on the a-, b-, or c-file.
- Progress. A friendly pawn on the opposing king's wing has relative rank 2 or higher. The opposing king's wing is the f-, g-, and h-files, or the a-, b-, and c-files, according to the foundation that matched.
- Completion. One of those pawns has relative rank 3 or higher, and a friendly rook or queen stands on a half-open file of that wing.
- Captures. The capturer is a pawn of that wing or a rook or queen on that wing. The victim stands on that wing.

Strategy 7: Fianchetto

Place a bishop on the long diagonal behind a pawn that has advanced one square, and aim the bishop at the center.

- Foundation. The b-pawn or the g-pawn has moved exactly one square, or the bishop already stands on b2, g2, b7, or g7, and a friendly bishop of the matching square color still exists.
- Progress. That bishop occupies the fianchetto square.
- Completion. That bishop geometrically attacks d4, d5, e4, or e5.
- Captures. The capturer is the fianchetto bishop or the pawn that opened its diagonal. The victim is a piece the bishop attacks.

Strategy 8: Open-File Control

Put a rook or queen on a file that contains no friendly pawn, and hold a fully open file.

- Foundation. Some file contains no friendly pawn, and a friendly rook or queen remains on the board.
- Progress. A friendly rook or queen stands on a file that contains no friendly pawn.
- Completion. A fully open file contains more friendly rooks and queens than opposing rooks and queens.
- Captures. The capturer is a rook or queen on a half-open file. The victim is a piece that major piece attacks.

Strategy 9: Seventh-Rank Invasion

Bring a rook or queen to relative rank 6 and attack an opposing unit there.

- Foundation. A friendly rook or queen exists, and at least one file contains no friendly pawn.
- Progress. A friendly rook or queen has relative rank 5 or higher.
- Completion. A friendly rook or queen has relative rank 6 and geometrically attacks an opposing unit. Relative rank 6 is White's seventh rank and Black's second rank.
- Captures. The capturer is a rook or queen on relative rank 5 or higher. The victim is a piece that piece attacks.

Strategy 10: Passed-Pawn Advance

Create a passed pawn, advance it, and defend it.

- Foundation. A friendly pawn has no opposing pawn on its file, or on an adjacent file, in front of it.
- Progress. That passed pawn has relative rank 3 or higher.
- Completion. A passed pawn has relative rank 5 or higher and is defended.
- Captures. The capturer is the passed pawn or a piece that defends it. The victim blocks it, attacks it, or stands on the square in front of it.

Strategy 11: Isolated-Pawn Attack

Attack an opposing pawn that has no neighbor of its own color.

- Foundation. The opponent has a pawn with no pawn of the opponent's own color on an adjacent file. The evaluated player's pawns do not decide whether that pawn is isolated.
- Progress. A friendly unit geometrically attacks that pawn, or a friendly rook or queen shares its file.
- Completion. The isolated pawn is geometrically attacked more times than it is defended.
- Captures. The capturer attacks the isolated pawn, or is a rook or queen sharing its file. The victim is the isolated pawn or a piece that defends it.

Strategy 12: Backward-Pawn Pressure

Restrain a pawn whose advance square is hit by a friendly pawn.

- Foundation. An opposing pawn's next square forward is attacked by a friendly pawn, no opposing pawn defends that advance square, and the pawn's file contains no friendly pawn.
- Progress. A friendly rook or queen shares that pawn's file.
- Completion. The advance square is occupied or attacked by the evaluated player, and the rook or queen remains on the file.
- Captures. The capturer is that major piece or a unit that attacks the advance square. The victim is the backward pawn or a piece that defends it.

Strategy 13: Knight Outpost

A knight occupies a square, on relative ranks 3 through 6, that a friendly pawn defends and no opposing pawn can attack.

- Foundation. Such a square exists. It is empty or already occupied by a friendly knight. An opposing pawn can still attack the square when that pawn stands on an adjacent file and its relative rank, measured for the opponent, is lower than the square's relative rank.
- Progress. A friendly knight occupies the square or geometrically attacks it.
- Completion. A friendly knight occupies the square.
- Captures. The capturer is the occupying knight or the pawn that defends the square. The victim is a piece the occupying knight attacks.

Strategy 14: Bishop Pair

Keep both bishops against fewer opposing bishops, and open a central file for them.

- Foundation. The evaluated player has two bishops and the opponent has fewer than two.
- Progress. At least one friendly bishop has left relative rank 0.
- Completion. A file from c through f contains no pawns, and a friendly bishop has at least three empty squares of travel along one single direction. The count is the longest one ray. It stops at the first occupied square. It is not the sum of every direction.
- Captures. The capturer is one of the two bishops. The victim is a piece that bishop attacks.

Strategy 15: Pin

A bishop, rook, or queen attacks an opposing piece that has a heavier opposing piece or the opposing king directly behind it.

Walk each ray from the sliding piece. Pieces are met in order. A candidate pair is two opposing pieces with no piece between them. The nearer is the front piece. The farther is the rear piece. The rear piece qualifies when it is the king or when it is strictly more valuable than the front piece.

- Foundation. A qualifying pair exists, and the stretch from the sliding piece to the front piece contains either no piece, or exactly one piece and that piece is opposing. If the first piece on the ray is friendly, that ray contains no pin. The rook on a1, the white pawn on a2, the black pawn on a7, and the rook on a8 are not a pin.
- Progress. No piece stands between the sliding piece and the front piece. The sliding piece geometrically attacks the front piece.
- Completion. Progress is true, and either the rear piece is the king or a second friendly unit also attacks the front piece.
- Captures. The capturer is the pinning piece or the second attacker. The victim is the front piece.

A friendly piece standing between the sliding piece and an opposing target is a discovered attack when Strategy 16 matches. It is not a pin.

Strategy 16: Discovered Attack

A friendly piece stands between a friendly sliding piece and an opposing target, and can move off that line.

- Foundation. The first piece on the sliding piece's ray is friendly, and the second piece on that ray is an opposing piece other than a pawn.
- Progress. That screening piece has a legal move that leaves the line.
- Completion. At least one such legal move captures or gives check, and after that move the sliding piece still attacks the original target. The score of 100 means the move is available on the position being scored. The move does not already have to have been played.
- Captures. The capturer is the sliding piece or the screening piece. The victim is the discovered target.

Strategy 17: Fork

One piece attacks two opposing pieces other than pawns, or a legal move reaches a square from which it would.

The two targets used for the value test are the two highest-valued opposing non-pawns attacked by that same piece. A target the moving piece would capture on the move being considered is not a target, because it would no longer stand there. A target meets the value test when it is a king, or it is not defended, or its value is at least the attacker's value.

- Foundation. One piece currently attacks two opposing non-pawns, or a legal move reaches a square from which that piece would attack two opposing non-pawns. For a bishop, rook, or queen, both targets must already meet the value test. For a knight or a pawn, any two opposing non-pawns are enough for the foundation.
- Progress. The same piece currently attacks both targets. A fork that exists only on a legal move to another square is foundation without progress. For a sliding piece, progress also requires the value test.
- Completion. The piece currently attacks both targets, and both meet the value test. This is the completion rule for knights and pawns as well as for sliding pieces. A knight is worth 320 and a pawn is worth 100. Every piece other than a pawn is worth at least 320. Therefore a knight or a pawn that currently attacks two opposing non-pawns is complete.
- Captures. The capturer is the forking piece. The victim is one of the forked pieces.

Strategy 18: Skewer

A sliding piece attacks a more valuable piece, with a lesser opposing piece directly behind it.

Use the same ray rule as Strategy 15. The front piece's value is at least the rear piece's value.

- Foundation. Such a pair exists, under the pin ray rule: the two opposing pieces are adjacent on the ray, and the stretch in front of the nearer piece is empty or contains exactly one opposing piece. A friendly first piece on the ray blocks the skewer.
- Progress. No piece stands between the sliding piece and the front piece, and the front piece is the king or is strictly more valuable than the rear piece.
- Completion. Progress is true, and either the front piece is the king, or the front piece is worth more than the sliding piece and the rear piece is undefended or worth at least as much as the sliding piece.
- Captures. The capturer is the sliding piece. The victim is the front piece or the rear piece.

Strategy 19: Major-Piece Battery

Two friendly rooks, or a friendly rook and queen, share a file or a rank.

- Foundation. The two pieces share a file or a rank, at most two pieces stand between them, and at least one of them has left its original square.
- Progress. At most one piece stands between them.
- Completion. Nothing stands between them, and they share a half-open file or one of them attacks an opposing piece along that line.
- Captures. The capturer is one of the two pieces. The victim is a piece that piece attacks.

Strategy 20: Promotion

Escort a pawn and give it a legal promoting move.

- Foundation. A friendly pawn has relative rank 4 or higher.
- Progress. A friendly pawn has relative rank 5 or higher.
- Completion. A friendly pawn has a legal move that promotes. A pawn on relative rank 6 that has no legal promoting move remains at progress. The pawn still belongs to the strategy. It is not fully developed until promotion is legal.
- Captures. The capturer is the advanced pawn or a piece that defends it. The victim attacks that pawn, stands in front of it, or can be taken by it. A promoting move that captures is eligible. A promoting move that does not capture is not a List 2 or List 4 row.

14.5 When the four lists are computed

All four lists use the recognition, scoring, and capture rules in this step.

- List 1 is computed on the current position for the evaluated player, whether or not it is that player's turn. Its future evidence looks one full turn ahead.
- List 2 is computed at the same time, on that same position. It has rows only when it is the evaluated player's turn. Otherwise it is empty.
- List 3 is computed only when it is the evaluated player's turn. It is computed separately after each legal move by that player. Its score looks one full turn ahead of that position.
- List 4 is computed after each legal opposing reply to a List 3 move. That is two individual moves after List 1's position, and it is again the evaluated player's turn. Recognition and capture pricing both run on that later position. Its score looks one full turn ahead of the reply.

============================================================
NEW REQUIREMENT — PERMANENT GAME ARCHIVES AND NEURAL-NETWORK
ASSISTANCE FOR LIST 1 AND LIST 3
============================================================

Purpose

The rolling White/Black history and future-search files are working files, not a complete lifetime record. By the end of a game, their history windows no longer contain the opening and most of the middlegame. Saving only these final windows is insufficient. The AI must be able to learn from earlier stages and use relevant experience when considering current and future strategies.

A. Optional recording at the start of a game

Before play begins, ask: "Add this game's records to the permanent AI chess archives? (Y/N)". Y records this game; N does not append this game's data. Either choice leaves normal game play, original file maintenance, and strategy evaluation functioning. N never clears or changes archived games. The selection is fixed for that game unless an explicit new-game action is taken.

B. Preserve information BEFORE the rolling windows are replaced

For games recorded with Y, capture an archival record at the time every confirmed move is processed, before the operational file's oldest record is removed, overwritten, or reset. Preserve the initial position and the pre-move and post-move states so the opening, middlegame, and endgame survive. Capture both players' move histories and the legal-move lists that the original program already records. Do not wait until the end of the game to save the surviving four-turn history window. At the end, append the outcome and mark the game's record complete; interrupted games remain recoverable and visibly incomplete.

C. Retain provenance and reconstruction information

Each archival record must identify the game, move number and side, position (or a faithful reconstruction method), legal move chosen, available legal moves before and after, the relevant current history window, and strategy-evaluation results where available. Record the 20 strategy identifiers, scores and supporting evidence where the engine provides them; also retain candidate-move/future-position associations needed to interpret List 3. Store the actual outcome when known, with engine/rules version and record version. Preserve the distinction between observed moves and hypothetical future moves: never mistake a searched branch for an actual played move. Do not fabricate values that the engine does not calculate.

D. Working files versus permanent archives

Keep the existing two side-specific history files and two future-search files, their formats, and their reset/update rules unless changes are explicitly approved. They must not be replaced by the archive. A new game may clear the four working files as specified, but must not clear the permanent archive. The permanent archive uses compatible representations of the source records where possible, with minimal game/position metadata to make them reconstructable. Splitting archives into additional same-format files when large is allowed; old segments must remain readable, and reading must cover all segments. File creation and rotation must never erase older records.

E. How List 1 uses archived experience

First run the authoritative chess engine's existing strategy recognition, legal-move checks, history window, and prescribed one-full-turn future analysis to produce List 1 and its official development stages/scores. Then permit an optional neural-network-assisted stage to retrieve or consume relevant archived examples and add an explicitly labeled *advisory* estimate for each recognized strategy: historical likelihood of progress/completion, confidence where measurable, and evidence/examples supporting the estimate. The official List 1 rows and scores (30, 45, 60, 75, 100, as applicable) are not silently rewritten using old game results. If the desired behavior is to re-rank List 1 by neural preference, provide a separate AI-assisted ordering alongside the original ordering. Archived examples alone do not establish that a strategy is currently present; that is decided by the 20 definitions using the current position and valid current-game history.

F. How List 3 uses archived experience

For each legal next move, construct the resulting legal board position and recognize forming strategies under the existing List 3 rules and the existing future-search depth. Keep all predictions associated with the candidate move and resulting position. An optional neural-network-assisted stage can use archives to compare similar historical positions, strategy progress, opponent replies, and eventual game outcomes and estimate the future prospects for each candidate. Display or store that estimate as advisory information beside List 3's existing score, with enough identifiers to link to the corresponding legal move. Do not substitute archived move sequences for legal move generation. Do not allow archive consultation to add extra plies or silently prune required legal branches.

G. Learning versus archive lookup

Reading an archive supports retrieval or comparisons; it does not, by itself, train a neural network. The future AI repository may train model weights from accumulated archived positions and outcomes, produce versioned checkpoints, and optionally offer inference to Lists 1 and 3. Keep recorded data, training, and inference separate. If no archive, trained checkpoint, or trustworthy similar example exists, Lists 1 and 3 must work normally and label AI advice unavailable. Do not treat archived success as proof that one particular strategy caused a win.

H. Information integrity and evaluation

Prevent accidental truncation, detect incomplete writes, preserve stable game identities, and avoid duplicate append on recovery. Make archived information available only from games completed or recorded before the current decision; do not leak outcomes or later moves of the current game into its own predictions. Maintain training/validation separation by complete game and compare AI-assisted rankings against unassisted ones on unseen games. Test the initial position, opening, middlegame, endgame, game restart, Y/N selection, resumed recording, file rollover, and cross-game archive retrieval. The existing legal chess rules, strategy definitions, capture valuation, and mandatory six-ply search-depth requirements must continue passing their original tests.

I. Implementation boundary

The immediate chess-engine update is to record complete selected games safely, without erasing the operational rolling-file behavior. Integrating and training the neural network is a separate, later stage in the fresh AI chess repository. The engine may expose a read-only interface for optional AI advice, but must still run correctly when that AI is absent. Before implementation, confirm whether neural advisory ranking is intended to remain separate or eventually become the main ordering of Lists 1 and 3; do not silently change official scores.
